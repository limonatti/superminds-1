🎧 Аудирование

Before the concert

Послушай диалог (озвучивается прямо в браузере), потом ответь на вопросы. Слушай сколько угодно раз.

Mia: Hi Leo! You look really worried. What's the matter?
Leo: Hi Mia. I'm so nervous. We're playing a concert tonight and I feel scared.
Mia: Scared? But you love being on stage!
Leo: I know, but this time it's different. There are three hundred tickets and they're all sold.
Mia: Wow, three hundred people! That's amazing, not scary.
Leo: For you, maybe. Last time I was so embarrassed — I forgot the words in the first song!
Mia: Ha! I remember. But honestly, everyone was still delighted. They were dancing and singing.
Leo: Really? I felt terrible. I was sure they were disappointed.
Mia: Not at all. My little brother said it was the best concert of his life.
Leo: That's surprising. I didn't know that.
Mia: See? You worry too much. Just breathe, relax and enjoy it.
Leo: You're right. When I start playing, I usually forget I'm nervous.
Mia: Exactly. And I'll be there in the front row, cheering for you.
Leo: Thanks, Mia. I'm feeling a bit calmer now. Let's do this!

🎧 Аудирование 2 (ещё один диалог)

Radio phone-in: How do you deal with stress?

Послушай второй диалог и ответь на вопросы. Слушай сколько нужно.

Presenter: Welcome back to the show. Today we're talking about stress with psychologist Dr Hill. Hello!
Dr Hill: Hello, thanks for having me.
Presenter: So, why do teenagers feel so stressed these days?
Dr Hill: There are many reasons — exams, friends, social media. Feeling nervous sometimes is completely normal.
Presenter: We have a caller. Nina, you're on the air!
Nina (caller): Hi! I get really nervous before exams and I can't sleep. What can I do?
Dr Hill: Good question, Nina. Talk to someone, breathe slowly, and go to bed early. A tired brain feels everything more strongly.
Presenter: Thanks, Nina. And we have Sam on line two.
Sam (caller): Hi. When I feel stressed, I just play video games all night. Is that bad?
Dr Hill: A little rest is fine, Sam, but too much screen time makes it worse. Try a short walk instead.
Presenter: Great advice. Any last tip, Dr Hill?
Dr Hill: Yes — be kind to yourself. Nobody feels confident all the time, and that's OK.

📕 Чтение

A festival to remember

Last summer I went to a music festival with my friends. Before the first concert I was really nervous, but I was also very excited. The weather was perfect and thousands of people were singing together.

Suddenly it started to rain, but nobody was upset. We were dancing in the rain and laughing. It was the best weekend of my life, and I felt so happy and relaxed. I'll never forget it!

📕 Чтение 2 (The science of a smile)

The science of a smile

Did you know that your face can change your mood? Scientists have found that when you smile — even a fake smile — your brain gets a small signal that says "I'm happy". After a few minutes, you often really do feel better.

It works the other way too. When we feel proud or excited, we stand taller and smile more. So feelings and the body are closely connected, like a two-way street.

Next time you feel a bit down, try this simple experiment: smile for thirty seconds. It won't solve big problems, but many people say it makes a stressful moment a little easier.

🎯 Exam skills · IELTS (формат экзамена)

Reading: Why do we have emotions?

Emotions are a normal and useful part of being human. Scientists believe that feelings like fear and happiness developed to help our ancestors survive. Fear, for example, warned early people about danger, while happiness encouraged them to repeat safe, helpful actions.

Today, our lives are safer, but our emotions work in the same way. When teenagers feel nervous before an exam, their body is simply getting ready to focus. The problem is not the feeling itself, but what we do with it. Research shows that people who talk about their emotions usually feel calmer than those who hide them.

Interestingly, emotions are not the same in every culture. In some countries, showing strong feelings in public is normal; in others, people prefer to stay calm. However, every culture has words for basic feelings such as anger, joy and sadness, which suggests that these emotions are shared by all humans.
