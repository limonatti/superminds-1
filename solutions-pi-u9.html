🎧 Аудирование

What's this gadget?

Послушай диалог (озвучивается прямо в браузере), потом ответь на вопросы. Слушай сколько угодно раз.

Zoe: Leo, look at this strange thing I found in the science lab. What is it?
Leo: Hmm. It's got a small screen and some buttons. It must be some kind of device.
Zoe: Could it be a phone?
Leo: It can't be a phone — there's no camera and it's too heavy.
Zoe: Good point. It's got wires coming out of the back.
Leo: Then it might be a machine that measures something.
Zoe: There's a label. It says 'used for measuring temperature'.
Leo: So it's a thermometer — a device which scientists use in experiments!
Zoe: Of course! The teacher whose class we're in probably left it here.
Leo: Let's give it back before someone thinks we took it.
Zoe: Ha, good idea. Science is like solving a mystery, isn't it?
Leo: Exactly. Every experiment is a little puzzle to solve.

🎧 Аудирование 2 (ещё один диалог)

Interview: a teenage inventor

Послушай второй диалог и ответь на вопросы. Слушай сколько нужно.

Host: Welcome! Today we're talking about young inventors with engineer Dr Nova. Hello!
Dr Nova: Hi! It's exciting to be here.
Host: So, can teenagers really invent useful things?
Dr Nova: Absolutely. Some of the best ideas come from young people who see problems adults ignore.
Host: We have a caller. Sam, go ahead.
Sam (caller): Hi! I love science but I'm not good at maths. Can I still be an inventor?
Dr Nova: Definitely, Sam. The most important skill is curiosity — the wish to ask 'why?' and 'what if?'.
Host: Any advice for a young person with an idea?
Dr Nova: Start small. Build a simple model, test it, and don't be afraid to fail. Every failure is a lesson which brings you closer.
Sam (caller): That's really encouraging. Thanks, Dr Nova!
Dr Nova: Good luck, Sam. The world needs your ideas!

📕 Чтение

The accident that helped the world

Some of the greatest discoveries happened by accident. In 1928, a scientist called Alexander Fleming was doing experiments with bacteria. One day he went on holiday and forgot to clean his lab.

When he came back, he noticed something strange: a mould which had grown in one dish had killed the bacteria around it. This mould became penicillin, the medicine that has saved millions of lives. Fleming later said that he didn't plan the discovery — he was simply a scientist who never ignored the small, strange things.

📕 Чтение 2 (Will robots take our jobs?)

Will robots take our jobs?

Robots and computers can already do many jobs. They build cars, answer phones and even help doctors. Some people are worried: will machines take all our jobs in the future?

Experts say the answer is complicated. Yes, robots will do many boring or dangerous tasks. But new technology also creates new jobs that didn't exist before. Someone has to design, build and repair the machines, and robots still can't do everything — they are not good at creativity or caring for people.

The best plan is to work with technology, not against it. If we learn new skills and stay curious, robots will be helpful tools, not enemies. The future belongs to people who keep learning.

✍️ Writing

Describe an invention. Выбери изобретение (телефон, интернет, колесо…) и напиши 7–8 предложений: что это (a device/machine that/which…), для чего используется (it's used for…), кто его придумал и почему оно важно. Используй relative clauses минимум 3 раза.

Проверь: who/which/that/where/whose; used for + -ing; -ful/-less прилагательные.

🎯 Exam skills · IELTS (формат экзамена)

Reading: Small ideas, big changes

When we think of great inventions, we often imagine huge machines or powerful computers. But some of the ideas that changed the world most were surprisingly small and simple.

Take the humble pencil, or the paper clip, or clean drinking water. None of these are exciting, yet each one made millions of lives easier and safer. The wheel, invented thousands of years ago, is still one of the most important ideas in history. What these inventions share is that they solved a real, everyday problem.

Scientists say that the best inventors are not always the cleverest people, but the most curious ones. They ask simple questions like "Why does this happen?" and "Is there a better way?" So the next time you notice a small, annoying problem, remember: it might be the start of a big idea.
